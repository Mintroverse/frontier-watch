Obsolescence check

Is the in-house work still the right bet?

Editorial assessment — judgment, not source data

Short answer: still the right bet — but the scarce skill just changed. Building agents is becoming a purchase; proving what they did is not for sale.

Six weeks compressed into one edition, and the shape is consistent. Capability got cheaper and more packaged: role-agents with job titles arrived as catalogue items, operators published agent counts as plans, and the connector standard gained the access control that governed buyers were waiting for. What did not get cheaper is assurance. The only measured study in this edition found twelve industrial companies stalled not on models but on verification, with human review as the sole trusted check. That is the work our own programme should be pricing, because nobody is selling it yet.

Validated by this edition

  • The connector posture held and improved: centrally managed agent access now runs through the identity provider a company already operates (S12), which removes the last structural objection to connecting AI to the system of record. Our validation work proceeds on live, governed rails.
  • The pilot discipline was vindicated from an unexpected direction. Human gates and evidence trails, which looked like caution, turn out to be exactly what four companies could not produce at scale (S09). Our template already has the shape; it now needs the volume question added.
  • The forecasting bet got its cleanest evidence yet — and a sharper design. There is no single best rented forecaster; the ranking reorders with the character of the series (S10), which makes our own history, split by behaviour, the right test rather than a vendor bake-off.

Watch — could overlap or absorb

  • Packaged role-agents now cover scope we might otherwise build (S01). That is mostly good news, but it means every build proposal needs a catalogue check first — and a cost-per-unit-of-work comparison, not a licence comparison.
  • Our own ERP's AI becomes purchasable at the next release with a consumption currency whose price is still unpublished (S08). An unpriced obligation inside the system of record is the largest open commercial exposure on this roadmap.
  • The freight premium has an expiry date now, and it cuts both ways: rates tied to construction fall away when sites finish (S06), while the air premium's year-on-year compression is a base effect rather than relief (S07). Budget the level, not the percentage.

    Method. Compiled Sep 17, 2026 from web research across analyst releases, primary vendor announcements, trade press and practitioner/academic sources. Impact (1–5) = editorial estimate of consequence for a mid-market supply chain organization if the signal holds. Adoptability (1–5) = editorial estimate of how directly a Fabric + Power BI + Acumatica stack can adopt or mimic it today. Multi-source = the substance is independently reported or evidenced by ≥2 unaffiliated organizations; publisher families count as one voice. Vendor-reported performance figures (agent counts, resolution rates, productivity and growth claims) are labeled as claims and not independently verified. Coverage notes this edition: (1) This edition covers six weeks, not one. Edition 06 was compiled Aug 6 (Week 32); no editions were published between then and this one, so the research window runs Aug 6 to Sep 17. Signal counts, pattern movements and organization tallies reflect six weeks of accumulation and are not comparable to a weekly delta — that caveat applies directly to the “all seven patterns advance” line in the pulse strip above.

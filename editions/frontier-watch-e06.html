Obsolescence check

Is the in-house work still the right bet?

Editorial assessment — judgment, not source data

Short answer: still the right bet — the week's AI winners are buying trucks, robots and optics, while the standards, benchmarks and price anchors our roadmap waited on keep arriving.

This edition's news was physical: the buildout showed up in truckload counts, warehouse M&A and robot fleets rather than model launches. For the in-house program, three long-standing waits moved in our favor — the connector standard's rails are carrying real first-party connectors, the demand-domain forecasting evidence finally exists, and the freight-exposure work was pointed at exactly the lanes that started moving — while newly surfaced price benchmarks sharpen, without settling, the build-versus-buy math.

Validated by this edition

  • The ask-your-vendor posture on connectors is now backed by market behavior: vertical platforms a fraction of Acumatica's size shipped official connectors within a week of the final (S12). The validation work proceeds on live rails, and ERP silence becomes an answer in itself.
  • The rented-forecaster bet cleared its evidence bar: a major retailer's own researchers found the rented baseline beat their production demand system (S06) — the deferred benchmark on our own POS history is now the obvious, low-risk next step.
  • The freight-exposure discipline was pointed at the right target: AI demand is now measurable in domestic trucking, not just trans-Pacific air (S02) — the planned exposure view covers exactly the lanes that started moving.

Watch — could overlap or absorb

  • The price anchors that arrived are independent estimates, not vendor terms (S13): they sharpen negotiation but settle nothing. Acumatica's AI Units terms at R2 remain the binding mid-market tell for what AI in our own stack will cost.
  • The buildout premium now sits under freight, warehouse space and components at once — and all of it deflates together if AI capital spending corrects. The first candidate data point exists (air spot softening month-on-month, S11); the exposure view is the hedge either way.
  • Physical automation is compounding at operators whose scale we rent, not match — the risk is drift toward their service bar without their unit economics. The scoreboard habit (cost per unit, hours reclaimed) is what keeps our pilots honest against theirs.

Obsolescence check

Is the in-house work still the right bet?

Editorial assessment — judgment, not source data

Short answer: still the right bet — and it becomes testable in seven days. On October 1 Acumatica either lets outside AI tools plug in through the industry-standard connection, or names a price, or both, or neither — and the likeliest case, a price without the connection, is the one the build plan should be written for.

The in-house work under review: governed, permissioned connections from our data platform to the ERP, and agent pilots that run under a human approval gate with an audit trail. One ordinary week, and the shape held. Capability keeps getting cheaper and more packaged: the Salesforce supply-chain agent named last edition turned out to be a process engine with thirty templates, Globality shipped autonomous sourcing with the negotiation inside, and the connector standard reached a consumer browser. What changed is who holds the door. Amazon decided which agents may transact on it, Salesforce built its own control layer for agents (a ‘harness’) and a screen-learning way around approved connectors, and five vendors began selling the controls — kill switches, rollbacks, run-time guards — that last edition said nobody offered. Our programme’s scarce skill is unchanged: proving what an agent did. It now has vendors to buy from and a date to plan around.

Validated by this edition

  • The verification gate was right and now has a market. Detection, containment and recovery arrived as three distinct product types this week (S06); our template already separates the human gate from the audit trail, and the only work is to add ‘who can stop it, and what does rollback return to’ before build.
  • The pricing discipline was vindicated by the first published demand curve: after HubSpot priced agents per result, actions rose 3.5 times and metered spend more than doubled (S02). Asking for the per-person, metered and per-result parts of a quote separately — this briefing’s standing advice — is exactly the question HubSpot’s own model now forces.
  • The connector bet held and gained reach: the standard shipped in Apple’s browser, under UN data and in a cannabis data product in one week (S12, S13). Our validation work on governed connectors is on the winning rail; the question is only whether Acumatica joins it on October 1.

Watch — could overlap or absorb

  • Salesforce’s catalogue now covers scope we planned to build. Agentforce Operations’ templates overlap the freight-invoice audit and order-intake pilots (S04); both need the build-versus-buy comparison before another hour is spent, and the integration route — approved connector or screen-learning agent — decided as policy.
  • The proprietary layer is real. Salesforce’s own agent control layer spanning third-party systems (S03), its agent that learns screens instead of using connectors (S04), and Amazon admitting agents on its own terms (S01) are the first vendor-scale instances of value moving around the open standard. Governed connectors remain our route; the exceptions need to be written down.
  • The unpriced exposure lands next week. An AI consumption currency inside Acumatica with no published price (S07) is the largest open commercial exposure on the roadmap — if Onematica is on a qualifying release at all, which is the first thing to confirm; October 1 either closes it or makes it a decision to defer AI features in the ERP until they are priced.
